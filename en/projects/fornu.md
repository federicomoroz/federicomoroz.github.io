## What it solves

A kitchen that sells through delivery and in the dining room usually juggles each platform's tablet, a notebook for phone orders, handwritten tickets, a spreadsheet for costs and a calculator to close the till. Fornu puts all of that in one place, and each person sees their part of it.

<ul class="benefits">
  <li><b>Every order on one board</b><span>Dining room, counter, your own delivery and the platforms, each with its status. New ones chime, and each station's tablet shows only its own work.</span></li>
  <li><b>Stock that takes care of itself</b><span>Every sale takes its recipe, options and packaging out of stock, and every purchase updates price and stock. The home page warns what is at its minimum and what runs out this week.</span></li>
  <li><b>What each dish leaves, per channel</b><span>The recipe with each ingredient at its last purchase price, and the margin on your own orders, Rappi, PedidosYa and the dining room. When an ingredient goes up, it tells you which prices fell short and what they should be.</span></li>
  <li><b>Sold out means sold out everywhere</b><span>One tap in the kitchen and it stops being offered in your own orders, in the linked platform stores and in the options made with it. The next day it comes back by itself.</span></li>
  <li><b>The till adds up, or says why not</b><span>Cash count per payment method, note by note if you like, blind if you choose. Each courier's cash is handed in with any difference written down.</span></li>
  <li><b>Everyone sees their part</b><span>Each person enters with their PIN on the place's tablet or phone, and their role decides what they see and do. What they can't do, someone else can authorize with their own PIN, for that one action.</span></li>
</ul>

## A shift, from every station

Every place works its own way: an order desk at the computer loading for the whole room, a waiter who is their own order desk, or waiters with phones. Fornu works for all three, by touch or by keyboard.

- **The order desk**, at the computer, opens tables in each waiter's name, finds products by name or types their code and <kbd>Enter</kbd>, and takes payment without leaving the keyboard.
- **The waiter**, on a phone or a tablet, seats the table, loads what is ordered and sends it to the kitchen. When the kitchen marks the round ready, it shows up as «Para servir» (ready to serve).
- **The kitchen**, on its tablet, sees only its own station, confirms, marks ready, and logs what was thrown away or prepared. If it prefers paper, a network thermal printer prints the tickets.
- **The courier**, on their phone, takes what is ready with **Me lo llevo** (I'm taking it), opens the address on the map, calls or messages on WhatsApp, and sees how much cash they carry.
- **The cashier** takes payment in full, by item or in equal parts, with tips and change, and opens and closes the cash count.

## What sets it apart

- **Each place switches on only what it uses.** Fornu is a base with modules: dining room, platforms, invoicing, staff, purchases, costs, reports and the floor plan builder. A module that is off does not appear on any screen, and what it saved is kept for when it is switched back on.
- **Margins are read per channel.** The same dish leaves a different amount in the dining room, in your own delivery and on each platform, and Fornu shows it next to the suggested price while you type the price.
- **Permissions can be lent.** A waiter who cannot void an item asks the cashier to authorize it with their PIN, for that one action, and Fornu records who authorized it.
- **The cash count can be blind.** Whoever counts the till does not see what the system expects until they confirm their count.
- **Nothing duplicates after a dropped connection.** A double tap or a retry on a bad connection never creates two orders, two lines or two payments.
- **Mistakes can be undone.** An order marked ready, a removed line or a paused product come back with one tap.

## For engineers

Fornu is multi-tenant: each account is a kitchen or a restaurant and sees only its own data. Every database query filters by the session's account and every write is stamped with it, so asking for another account's record answers exactly as if it did not exist.

<div class="chip-row">
  <span class="tag">C#</span><span class="tag">.NET 8</span><span class="tag">ASP.NET Core</span><span class="tag">EF Core</span>
  <span class="tag">PostgreSQL</span><span class="tag">SignalR</span><span class="tag">React 19</span><span class="tag">TypeScript</span>
  <span class="tag">Vite</span><span class="tag">Docker</span><span class="tag">xUnit</span><span class="tag">Testcontainers</span>
</div>

**A base, and modules that don't know each other.** The application is built in layers: the platform (accounts, sessions, permissions, the module registry), the core (menu, stock items and stock, orders, kitchen tickets, cash, team), the capabilities (dining room, platform delivery, purchasing, staff, invoicing), the services that read what happened (costs, reports), and the packs, which are data only. Each module is its own project and references only the application layer. Its adapter brings its tables, repositories and routes. Architecture tests read the `.csproj` files and fail if a module names another module or if the core names a module.

**They talk through a mediator, the dock.** Between layers nobody calls an object: they send a message. There are requests (one answers), queries (one, several or none answer) and facts already saved (zero or more listen). In one place the dock applies the switched-off-module lock, the account, the permissions, the time limit and fault isolation, and logs every message. Each module has a boss that presents its card (layer, permissions, suggested roles, channels) and is the only piece that couples it to the dock. A module that is off answers nothing: not its routes, its background jobs or its webhooks.

**Integrations wait in a queue.** Rappi orders arrive through signed webhooks and are stored as received before being processed. Confirming, rejecting and marking ready go out through an outbox with retries, so the kitchen never waits for the platform. PedidosYa comes in through its POS plugin, with JWT. An invoice is saved with the order, and a separate queue asks ARCA for the CAE, in order and one at a time, with the same ARCA client that [ArcaSim]({{ '/en/projects/arcasim/' | relative_url }}) ships. Both platforms and ARCA were developed against simulators that answer in their formats.

| Part | Built with |
|---|---|
| Backend | C# · .NET 8 · ASP.NET Core · EF Core 8 with Npgsql · SignalR for live updates |
| Web | React 19 · TypeScript · Vite, served by the same process as the API |
| Data | PostgreSQL, one database for every account, filtered by account |
| Integrations | Rappi (webhooks with inbox/outbox), PedidosYa (POS plugin), ARCA (WSAA and WSFEv1), ESC/POS thermal printers over TCP through a local agent |
| Deployment | One Docker image with the web and the API, on Render, with its PostgreSQL |
| Tests | xUnit against real PostgreSQL with Testcontainers, architecture tests, and web tests with `node --test` |

The last full run (November 10, 2026) was 629 server tests and 181 web tests, all green, plus 61 end-to-end walks in a real browser, each on a fresh database, without an error. The API tests start a real PostgreSQL with the demo account and a second account next to it, to check that neither can reach the other's data. The printing tests run the real agent against the server, and the invoicing tests ask an in-memory ArcaSim for their CAEs.

The code is private. The public documentation, with the layer map, is at [github.com/federicomoroz/fornu-docs](https://github.com/federicomoroz/fornu-docs).
